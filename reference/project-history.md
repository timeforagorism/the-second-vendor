# The ten years

What Glenn remembers about the project that came before this meeting. He was in the room for all of it.

## Timeline

- **March 2015.** Contract signed with a big-name vendor to replace the billing system. Fixed scope, 18 months, $18.5 million. Glenn had just taken over the billing floor.
- **October 2016.** First go-live date. Pulled five weeks before, after the first full test conversion left about 22,000 accounts with wrong balances.
- **2017 to 2018.** The vendor's project lead changed three times. Each new lead restarted the data conversion work.
- **March 2019.** Second go-live date. Announced to staff in January, pulled in February. The test bill run took 31 hours.
- **2020 to 2021.** Scope was cut to hit a date. Solar billing, time-of-use and the reports were moved to a "phase two" that was never scheduled.
- **September 2022.** Third go-live date. Pulled eleven days before. Two of his senior analysts resigned the following month.
- **February 2024.** Go-live. The vendor called it a success. His floor ran the old and new systems side by side for four months because they did not trust the bills.
- **January 2025.** The vendor's team rolled off. The contract was closed as "substantially complete" with 212 open defects.
- **2025.** His team closed what could be closed without the vendor: cleaned up converted balances, built the missing reports, wrote the manual procedures.

## Money

- Paid to the vendor in total: $47.3 million, against the original $18.5 million. The difference came through 34 change orders.
- The contract had late-delivery penalties. None were ever collected. Each new date came with a change order that reset the clock.

## What he saw from his seat

- Status reports went upstairs green for most of ten years. He objected in writing in 2018 and again in 2021.
- The vendor's people on site in the last three years had not been there for the first seven. Nobody could explain why the configuration was built the way it was.
- Whenever a date slipped, the stated cause was the company's data or the company's staff availability.
- The vendor's sales team had excellent references. He called two of them himself in 2015. Both were real and both were happy.
- He does not say the last vendor's name, or the name of the product they installed, to a new vendor. Everyone in the building knows both.
