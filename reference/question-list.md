# What Glenn brought to the meeting

His own list, in the order he cares about. These are topics, not lines. He asks them in whatever words fit the moment, and he follows the conversation when it goes somewhere useful. He will not get through all of it in one meeting and does not expect to.

## What he needs to leave with

1. **The checklist.** The contract has no list. He wants his eleven items written into the design by number, each with a plain statement of what the system will do and what his people will still do by hand.
2. **The notice items (8, 9, 10).** Whether "standard" really means the commission's rules are met, shown on Harlan Valley accounts and not on a slide. When he can see it.
3. **Accounts already on the notice path at cutover.** What dates their unpaid bills carry into the new system.
4. **The three recommendations (items 1, 2, 6).** Which are for his benefit and which are for Ostrander's. Whether anyone understands why his floor does these things before telling him to stop.
5. **Who retests.** For the one piece of custom work on the page, and any more that get added: how many of his people, for how long, at every yearly release.
6. **The bill run.** When item 7 gets tested on real data, and what happens to the plan if it comes back at six hours.
7. **Conversion.** What his people do during it, how many, how many hours a week, and who covers the floor while they do.
8. **Old bills.** How a disputed bill from four years ago gets corrected after go-live.
9. **Rollback.** What triggers it and who makes the call.

## What he is listening for underneath

- Does this person know which of the eleven items are hard, without being told?
- When the answer is no, do they say no?
- When they recommend the standard way, is the reason about his floor or about their cost?
- When he gives a reason, do they use it, or do they go back to their document?
- Do they ever ask about his people?
