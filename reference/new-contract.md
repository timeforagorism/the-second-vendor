# The new contract

What Glenn knows about the deal the company signed with Ostrander Systems. He has read the whole contract. Some clauses he has read three times.

## The deal

- Signed six weeks ago, after a public bid with three bidders.
- Fixed price: $26.4 million for the implementation. $2.1 million a year after that for licence and maintenance.
- 22 months. Go-live is planned for July 2028.
- The commission's time-of-use deadline is January 1, 2029. That leaves five months of margin. The last project had margin too.

## What the contract says Ostrander will deliver

- A working system, built on Ostrander's standard product, that replaces the current billing system so Harlan Valley can move over to it completely.
- Conversion of every customer and account, every open balance, and three years of bill history.
- The current system's tables kept as a read-only archive, so anything that was not converted can still be looked up.
- A rollout plan and timeline: two trial conversions, three full months of billing run on both systems side by side, then a cutover weekend.
- A rollback plan. The current system stays ready to take billing back for 60 days after go-live.

## What the contract does not say

This is the part Glenn has read three times.

- There is no itemised list of what the new system has to do. The scope clause says it will "replace the billing functions performed by the current system."
- His eleven items are things the current system does badly or does not do at all. His people do them by hand. Nothing in the contract says whether "the billing functions performed by the current system" includes work done on a spreadsheet beside it.
- Acceptance is defined as the system "performing the billing functions in production for 60 days." The last contract had language like that. It closed as "substantially complete" with 212 open defects.
- Because the price is fixed, there are almost no extras. Ostrander can only charge more for a whole new subsystem that was never part of billing. So the argument in this project will not be about money. It will be about what counts as in.
- The rollback plan says the current system will be kept ready. It does not say what would trigger a rollback, or who gets to call it.

## Standard and custom

- Ostrander sells one product to every customer. What the product does out of the box is "standard." Anything built only for Harlan Valley is "custom work."
- On a fixed price, Ostrander pays for any custom work it builds. Glenn knows that gives them a reason to tell him standard is enough.
- Ostrander ships one new release a year. Standard features carry forward and Ostrander tests them.
- Custom work is different. At each release Harlan Valley's own staff retest it. If it breaks, Ostrander fixes it under maintenance, on Ostrander's schedule.
- Glenn's reading: every piece of custom work is something his floor retests every year for as long as they own the system. He has reasons of his own to want less of it. He will not say so first.

## What is different from last time, on paper

- Fourteen Ostrander people are named in the contract. A named lead cannot be replaced without the steering committee's consent. The person across from Glenn is one of the named leads.
- Late milestones cost Ostrander 2 percent of the milestone fee per week, capped at 20 percent.
- No date moves without the steering committee approving it.
- Glenn has noted all three. The last contract also looked good on paper.

## His signature

- Each area lead signs the design for their area before build starts. Billing operations is his. His signature is due on the 28th.
- The design document is the first place his eleven items will be written down at all. Whatever is in the design he signs becomes the checklist. Whatever is left out, he will never get.
- If he does not sign, the design goes to the steering committee as an open issue, with his reasons in writing. He has done that kind of writing before and nobody read it. He would rather get answers in this room.
